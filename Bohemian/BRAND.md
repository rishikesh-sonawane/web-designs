# PHASES 2–3 — BRAND & WORLD

## The one world: *Vintage Atelier / Mediterranean Bohemian*

Per Plan §3 we choose **one coherent interpretation**: an old, sun-bleached
ceramics atelier remembered through the goods of print — parchment, ink,
stamps, catalog cards. Everything bows to that world: color, type, texture,
decoration, motion. No dreamcatchers, no generic mandalas, no floating leaves
(Plan §77).

---

## Casa Arcilla — Taller de Cerámica (House of Clay)

**Tagline:** *Clay, fire & patience — since 1927.*

**What it is:** a family ceramics atelier in the cliff town of **Ronda,
Andalucía** — a courtyard workshop where vessels, tableware, tiles and
botanical planters are thrown, glazed and fired by hand. Also a school
(workshops) and a commission studio.

**Founded:** 1927, by **Concepción "Concha" Solares**, a potter who carried
the Talavera methods of her hometown into a small kiln by the Tagus gorge.

**The lineage (the history):**
- **1927** — Concha throws her first *botija* at the Taller; her glazes are
  white-earth and cobalt, Talavera tradition.
- **1954** — Daughter **Isabel** takes the wheel. She pulls the palette into
  the garden — sage, mustard, the ochre of dry Andalusian hills.
- **1978** — A three-year exchange with **Oaxacan barro negro** masters
  teaches the Taller a black-clay language and a new patience with fire.
- **2001** — Great-granddaughter **Amparo** returns from Sado, Japan with
  *raku* in her hands and founds the **Triple Fire** method — a firing that
  honors all three lineages.
- **Hoy** — Amparo and twelve makers keep the courtyard kiln alight; every
  piece carries a number, a date and a signature.

**Personality (Plan §5):** Grounded · Warm · Quietly Bold · Curious · Devoted · Artful.

**Audience:** design-conscious hosts and galleries, slow-living collectors,
workshop seekers, commissions from small hotels and restaurants.

**Voice:** first-person plural, precise and warm — the maker's notebook, not
the ad. Seasoned with Spanish: *fuego lento* (slow fire), *el sol* (the sun),
*la memoria* (memory). Catalog metadata: piece numbers, kiln numbers, dates,
provenance — museum grammar without the museum.

**Value proposition:** one-of-a-kind objects with lineage and provenance;
honest process; slow-fire integrity; pieces made to be used.

**CTA vocabulary (Plan §61):** *Visit the Taller · Meet the makers · Join a
workshop · Start a commission · Fire with us · Read the Archive.*

## Signature visual device (Plan §76): **the sun (*el sol*)**

One recurring mark — a hand-drawn sun with short rays. It appears:
- rising in the hero arch,
- as a stamp on provenance cards and the footer,
- as the section divider glyph,
- drawn-in (SVG stroke) once per page view.

Everything decorative on this site is either the sun, the arch, or quarter-tone
linen. That's the whole decorative language.

## Signature interaction (earmark for Phase 11): **The Glaze Lab**

Plan §32's *interactive material explorer*: a palette of historical glaze
recipes (Talavera white, Verde de Ronda, Barro Negro, Sado Raku). Choosing one
re-skins a live canvas vessel on the page and warms the surrounding surface — a
creation ritual, deliberately distinct from Bento-Grid's rollback sequence.

## Why this is historical & artistic (not "boho")

- **History** is structural: a founder, a place, three generations, three
  ceramic lineages (Spain · Mexico · Japan). The narrative does the adorning.
- **The artistic touch** is editorial: parchment + ink + stamps + catalog
  cards + a single hand-drawn sun, instead of emoji and dried flowers.
- It survives Plan §79's *"too boho" test*: strip every decoration and the
  typography + layout + story still stand.
